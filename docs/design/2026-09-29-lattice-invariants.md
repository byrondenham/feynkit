# Lattice invariants

Design note for a module `feynkit/lattice_invariants.py` that computes the classical lattice
invariants of a lattice polytope exactly and in pure Python, for a method
`FeynmanIntegral.lattice_invariants`, and for their place in the analysis report and in
`fk analyse`.

## Purpose

`polytope_data` gives the face lattice, the facets and the normalised volume of the Newton
polytope $P$ of $G$. The volume is the holonomic rank of the GKZ system for generic $\beta$
(section 4.5 of the mathematics reference). Whether it is the rank for every $\beta$ depends on
the monoid $\mathbb{N}A$ generated by the columns $(1, \alpha_j)$ of $A$: when it is normal,
$\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]$ is Cohen-Macaulay (Hochster 1972), and then the rank does not jump
(Matusevich, Miller and Walther 2005). Nothing in feynkit tests normality. The module computes:

- the lattice points and interior lattice points of $kP$, listed or counted;
- the Ehrhart polynomial $L_P(k) = \#(kP \cap \Lambda)$ and the $h^*$-vector;
- the Gorenstein index, whether $P$ is reflexive, and the polar dual of a reflexive $P$;
- the lattice width with a direction that attains it;
- the integer decomposition property (IDP), whether the support holds every lattice point of
  $P$, and so whether $\mathbb{N}A$ is normal.

Everything is exact: integers and `Fraction`s throughout, facets from the certified hull of
`feynkit._exact`. No floating point is used. An optional Normaliz backend, found at run time as
Singular and 4ti2 are, decides IDP for large polytopes; the pure-Python path is the reference.

## Lattices

Two lattices are natural for $P = \operatorname{conv}(\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_N)$ of dimension
$d$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$:

- the support lattice $\alpha_1 + L$, with $L$ spanned by the differences $\alpha_j - \alpha_1$.
  It is the lattice of `normalized_volume`, of the chart $x = o + Bc$ of `lattice_chart`, and of
  $\mathbb{Z}A$, and so the one in which normality of $\mathbb{N}A$ is defined;
- the ambient lattice $\operatorname{aff}(P) \cap \mathbb{Z}^n = \alpha_1 + \operatorname{sat}(L)$,
  the usual lattice of Ehrhart theory.

They agree when `sublattice_index`, $[\operatorname{sat}(L) : L]$, is 1, which holds for the
Newton polytopes of the graphs `generate_graphs` gives with one loop and up to six legs, or two
loops and up to four, all at generic kinematics. `lattice="support"` is the default
and `lattice="ambient"` an option. In either case $P$ is written in a chart $x = o + Sc$, where the
columns of $S$ are the Hermite-normal-form basis of $L$ (the chart of `lattice_chart`) or of
$\operatorname{sat}(L)$, and $P$ becomes a full-dimensional polytope in $\mathbb{Z}^d$. The basis of
$\operatorname{sat}(L)$ comes from the Hermite normal form $B^T W = [0 \mid H]$ already used for
the frame of section 7.3 of the mathematics reference: $B H^{-T}$ is a basis of
$\operatorname{sat}(L)$, brought into Hermite normal form. The chart is public as
`invariant_chart`, since the width direction and the polar dual are given in its coordinates. The
dilate $kP$ has the lattice $k o + S\mathbb{Z}^d$.

The normality of $\mathbb{N}A$ is a property of the support lattice. `LatticeInvariants.normal`
is computed there whatever lattice is chosen; the other fields belong to the chosen lattice.

## Methods

### Lattice points

For $j = 1, \ldots, d$ let $P_j$ be the projection of $P$ to the first $j$ chart coordinates,
the hull of the projected vertices, with facets $a \cdot y \le b$ from beneath-beyond, certified
complete. $P_1$ is an interval and $P_d = P$. A point $c$ lies in $kP$ exactly when each prefix
$(c_1, \ldots, c_j)$ lies in $kP_j$. The enumerator walks the prefixes: given one in $kP_j$, the
facets of $P_{j+1}$ with $a_{j+1} \ne 0$ bound $c_{j+1}$ by
$$a_{j+1} c_{j+1} \le k b - \sum_{i \le j} a_i c_i,$$
evaluated with integer floor division. A facet with $a_{j+1} = 0$ is a facet of $P_j$ and holds
already. Every prefix in $kP_j$ extends to a real point of $kP_{j+1}$, so the walk visits only
prefixes of real points, and a prefix whose range holds no integer ends there. At the last
coordinate the integers of the range are counted, not listed.

Interior points use the same walk with strict inequalities at every level, $a \cdot y < kb$
becoming $a \cdot y \le kb - 1$ in integers. This is exact because the projection maps the
interior of $kP_{j+1}$ onto that of $kP_j$, and a facet of $P_{j+1}$ with $a_{j+1} = 0$ is a facet
of $P_j$, which the prefix already satisfies strictly. One walk counts both: it runs over the
closed ranges and carries whether the prefix is interior.

A rational dilation factor $k = p/q$ is allowed internally, with $q a \cdot y \le p b$; the width
search below uses it.

### Ehrhart polynomial and $h^*$-vector

$L_P(k)$ is a polynomial of degree $d$ with $L_P(0) = 1$ and leading coefficient
$\mathrm{vol}(P)/d!$, the volume normalised in the chosen lattice (Beck and Robins 2015). By
Ehrhart-Macdonald reciprocity $L_P(-k) = (-1)^d L_{P^\circ}(k)$, the number of interior points of
$kP$. One walk of $kP$ for each $k = 1, \ldots, \lceil d/2 \rceil$ gives both counts, and so values
at $0, \pm 1, \ldots, \pm \lceil d/2 \rceil$. The polynomial is interpolated in `Fraction`s from
$L_P(0)$, the leading coefficient and the first $d$ values in the order
$L_P(1), L_P(-1), L_P(2), L_P(-2), \ldots$, which needs dilates up to about $d/2$ only. The values
left over, one or two, are checked against it. The volume comes from the pulling triangulation of
the certified face lattice in the chart.

The $h^*$-vector is defined by $\sum_{k \ge 0} L_P(k) t^k = h^*(t)/(1 - t)^{d+1}$, so
$h^*_i = \sum_{j=0}^{i} (-1)^j \binom{d+1}{j} L_P(i - j)$. Its entries are non-negative integers,
$h^*_0 = 1$, $h^*_1 = L_P(1) - d - 1$, $h^*_d = L_{P^\circ}(1)$ and
$\sum_i h^*_i = \mathrm{vol}(P)$; each is checked, and a failure raises `ComputationError` as a
bug. The vector has $d + 1$ entries, trailing zeros included.

### Gorenstein index, reflexivity and the polar dual

Write the facets as $m_F \cdot c \le b_F$ with $m_F$ primitive in $\mathbb{Z}^d$. $P$ is
Gorenstein of index $r$ when $rP$ has a lattice point $p$ at lattice distance 1 from every facet:
$$r\, b_F - m_F \cdot p = 1 \quad \text{for every facet } F.$$
The homogenised forms $(b_F, -m_F)$ span $\mathbb{Q}^{d+1}$, since the cone over $P$ is pointed
and full-dimensional, so the system has at most one rational solution $(r, p)$. $P$ is Gorenstein
exactly when that solution exists and is integral; $r$ is then positive, since
$\sum_F \lambda_F m_F = 0$ with $\lambda_F > 0$ rules out $r \le 0$. $P$ is reflexive when $r = 1$
(Batyrev 1994). Its polar dual, in the dual lattice, is
$$P^* = \{y : y \cdot (c - p) \ge -1 \text{ for every } c \in P\} = \operatorname{conv}(-m_F),$$
whose vertices are the $-m_F$. $P^*$ is again reflexive and $(P^*)^* = P - p$. The vertices of
$P^*$ alone may span a proper sublattice, as those of the cube $[-1, 1]^d$ do, so the invariants of
$P^*$ are to be computed with `lattice="ambient"`.

### Lattice width

The width of $P$ in the direction $u \in \mathbb{Z}^d \setminus \{0\}$ is
$w_u = \max_P u \cdot c - \min_P u \cdot c$, and the lattice width is the least $w_u$. Let $w_0$
be the least width over the coordinate directions and the facet normals; when $w_0 = 1$, the least
possible, the search stops at once. For $G = U + F$ the monomials of $U$ lie on
$\sum_e x_e = L$ and those of $F$ on $\sum_e x_e = L + 1$, so when those of $U$ span a facet, its
normal has width 1. Every $u$ with $w_u \le w_0$ satisfies
$|u \cdot (v - v')| \le w_0$ for all vertices $v, v'$, so it lies in $w_0 (P - P)^\circ$, the
dilate of the polar of the difference body $P - P$. With the facets $g_G \cdot x \le h_G$ of
$P - P$ (integral, $h_G > 0$), that polar is $\operatorname{conv}(g_G / h_G)$. Scaling by the least
common multiple $D$ of the $h_G$ gives the integer polytope $Q = \operatorname{conv}(D g_G / h_G)$,
and the candidates are the non-zero lattice points of $(w_0/D) Q$, which the enumerator lists
with a rational factor. The search is complete, and returns the least width with the first
direction attaining it: the coordinate directions first, then the facet normals and then the
candidates, each in lexicographic order and with its first non-zero entry positive.

### IDP and normality

$P$ has the integer decomposition property when every lattice point of $kP$ is a sum of $k$
lattice points of $P$. By Bruns, Gubeladze and Trung (1997) the lattice points of $(k+1)P$ are
sums of those of $kP$ and of $P$ for every $k \ge d - 1$, so IDP holds exactly when this is so for
$k = 1, \ldots, d - 2$. The check lists $(k+1)P$ and looks up $x - y$ in the set of points of $kP$
for each lattice point $y$ of $P$. Every polytope of dimension at most 2 has IDP.

$\mathbb{N}A$ is normal when it equals $\operatorname{cone}(A) \cap \mathbb{Z}A$. At degree $k$ the
right-hand side is the set of lattice points of $kP$ in the support lattice, and the left the sums
of $k$ support points. So $\mathbb{N}A$ is normal exactly when the support holds every lattice
point of $P$ and $P$ has IDP, both in the support lattice. Then $\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]$ is
Cohen-Macaulay (Hochster 1972). When $P$ is full-dimensional, $A$ has full row rank and the
holonomic rank is the normalised volume for every $\beta$ (Matusevich, Miller and Walther 2005).
Below full dimension the GKZ system has no non-zero solutions for generic $\beta$, and only the
Cohen-Macaulay statement is made. A monoid that is not normal can still be Cohen-Macaulay, so a
negative answer settles nothing.

### Normaliz backend

`backend="normaliz"` writes the chart vertices as a Normaliz `polytope` input with the goal
`HilbertBasis` and reads the numbers of Hilbert basis elements and of degree-1 elements from the
`.inv` file: $P$ has IDP exactly when they agree, and the degree-1 count is checked against
feynkit's. Normaliz computes in exact integer arithmetic. It is found with
`shutil.which("normaliz")`; `backend="auto"` uses it when present and falls back to Python when it
is missing or fails, and `backend="normaliz"` raises `ComputationError` instead.

### Work budget

The walk for the Ehrhart polynomial and the IDP check grow fast with $d$: for the massless
three-loop box `112|3|34|5|6e|7e|7e|e|:zzzzzzzzzz`, of dimension 10, the check would take about
$2 \times 10^{10}$ set lookups and hold the $1.3 \times 10^8$ lattice points of $9P$, and for its
massive version the walk alone takes 35 s. `lattice_invariants` takes `budget`, the most steps of
pure-Python work, where a step is one term of an inequality the walk evaluates or one set lookup
of the IDP check; CPython takes about four million of either a second. The walk counts its steps
and stops when the budget runs out. The IDP check needs
$|P \cap \mathbb{Z}^d| \sum_{k=2}^{d-1} L_P(k)$ lookups at most, known from the Ehrhart
polynomial before it starts, and holds at most $L_P(d-1)$ points. Over budget, Normaliz supplies
the Ehrhart polynomial from its Hilbert quasipolynomial, checked against the volume and against
$L_P(1)$ and $L_{P^\circ}(1)$ from the walk, and the $h^*$-vector against its Hilbert series;
without Normaliz the field is None, and so are `idp` and `normal`, except that `normal` is False
whenever the support misses a lattice point. `timeout` limits each run of Normaliz, and with
`backend="auto"` a failure falls back to Python only within the budget. Without a budget, the
default, everything is computed as before. The report and `fk analyse` pass $2 \times 10^7$ steps,
about five seconds, and a timeout of 60 s.

## Interfaces

```python
# feynkit/lattice_invariants.py
Lattice = Literal["support", "ambient"]

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class LatticeInvariants:
    lattice: Lattice
    dimension: int
    lattice_points: int
    interior_points: int
    ehrhart: tuple[Fraction, ...] | None  # c_0, ..., c_d of L_P(k); None over budget
    h_star: tuple[int, ...] | None        # h*_0, ..., h*_d; None over budget
    gorenstein_index: int | None        # None when P is not Gorenstein
    reflexive: bool
    lattice_width: int
    width_direction: tuple[int, ...]    # in the coordinates of invariant_chart
    idp: bool | None                    # None over budget
    support_is_saturated: bool          # the support holds every lattice point of P
    normal: bool | None                 # NA normal, in the support lattice; None over budget

def invariant_chart(points, *, lattice="support") -> LatticeChart
def lattice_points(points, k=1, *, interior=False, lattice="support") -> list[tuple[int, ...]]
def count_lattice_points(points, k=1, *, interior=False, lattice="support") -> int
def ehrhart_polynomial(points, *, lattice="support") -> sp.Poly
def h_star_vector(points, *, lattice="support") -> tuple[int, ...]
def gorenstein_index(points, *, lattice="support") -> int | None
def polar_dual(points, *, lattice="support") -> tuple[tuple[int, ...], ...] | None
def lattice_width(points, *, lattice="support") -> tuple[int, tuple[int, ...]]
def is_idp(points, *, lattice="support", backend="auto", timeout=None) -> bool
def lattice_invariants(
    points, *, lattice="support", backend="auto", budget=None, timeout=None
) -> LatticeInvariants
```

`points` is anything `polytope_data` accepts. `lattice_points` returns ambient coordinates, sorted.
A point ($d = 0$) has one lattice point in every dilate, Ehrhart polynomial 1, $h^* = (1)$, is
reflexive with dual $\{()\}$, has width 0 in the empty direction, and has IDP.

`FeynmanIntegral.lattice_invariants(lattice="support", backend="auto")` calls
`lattice_invariants` on the points of the Newton polytope.

## Report and CLI

The report's `Polytope` gains `invariants`, last and None by default, filled with
`fi.lattice_invariants()` in the support lattice. The Newton polytope section gives the lattice
points, the $h^*$-vector, the lattice width and the Gorenstein index, and ends with the
certificate: "$\mathbb{N}A$ is normal, $\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}A]$ is Cohen-Macaulay and there are
no rank jumps", with the rank clause only in full dimension, or with the reasons $\mathbb{N}A$ is
not normal and the remark that this decides nothing. The summary gains `Lattice points`,
`Interior lattice points`, `Gorenstein index` and `Normal configuration`, and so does the JSON of
`fk analyse --json`; `fk analyse -n` prints the same with the $h^*$-vector, the width and IDP.
Every invariant is shown by default: all of them take under 0.2 s on the kite, the pentagon and
the hexagon. Both compute them once, through the cache of `FeynmanIntegral.lattice_invariants`,
within `LATTICE_BUDGET` and `NORMALIZ_TIMEOUT` of `feynkit.io.report`, and say "not computed"
for a field left None, with the advice to install Normaliz or call `lattice_invariants`
directly.

the Hilbert basis of the cone over $P$ instead. `fk analyse` on the three-loop graph takes 11 s in
all without the invariants.

## Testing

- Textbook cases: the unit simplex, $L(k) = \binom{k+d}{d}$; the unit cube, $L(k) = (k+1)^d$; the
  cross-polytope, reflexive with the cube as dual; the Reeve tetrahedra on $(0,0,0)$, $(1,0,0)$,
  $(0,1,0)$, $(1,1,r)$, with $L(k) = \frac{r}{6} k^3 + k^2 + (2 - \frac{r}{6}) k + 1$,
  $h^* = (1, 0, r - 1, 0)$ and no IDP for $r > 1$; the configuration $A = \{0, 1, 3, 4\}$, whose
  support misses 2, so that $\mathbb{N}A$ is not normal (the example of a rank jump).
- Both lattices on configurations whose sublattice index is above 1, below and in full dimension.
- Brute force on random polytopes of dimension at most 3, by membership over a box: lattice and
  interior counts of several dilates, and the width over all directions in a box that contains
  every candidate.
- Every returned polar dual is reflexive and its dual is $P - p$; every width direction attains
  the width; the Ehrhart polynomial agrees with direct counts beyond the interpolated dilates.
- Oracles at test time, skipped when absent: the Normaliz binary (Ehrhart series, $h^*$, interior
  points by reciprocity, Gorenstein index, IDP and integral closure of the support monoid) and
  Sage's `LatticePolytope` (reflexivity, polar dual) on the one-loop graphs of `generate_graphs`
  with up to six legs, and on a sample of the two-loop graphs with up to four legs under the
  `slow` marker. No table of invariants of Feynman polytopes is stored in the tests.

## Changes to core modules

`integral.py` gains the method `lattice_invariants`, in a commit of its own. `polytope.py` and the
other core modules are unchanged; the new module uses `lattice_chart` and the certified hull of
`feynkit._exact`.

## Decisions

- The support lattice is the default. It is the lattice of the volume, of $\mathbb{Z}A$ and of
  normality; for the Newton polytopes of generated graphs at generic kinematics the two lattices
  agree.
- Enumeration by projections needs no linear programming and stays in integers. The projections
  are computed once per polytope and shared by every dilate.
- The polynomial is interpolated from dilates up to $\lceil d/2 \rceil$ with reciprocity, and the
  spare values are checked, rather than counted up to $d$.
- `normal` is always the normality of $\mathbb{N}A$, in the support lattice, since that is what
  the Cohen-Macaulay certificate needs.
